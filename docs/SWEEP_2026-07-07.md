## Strand 1 — Format vs content research

| Source | What it claims (unverified) |
|---|---|
| arXiv **2602.21223** (Feb 2026) "Measuring Pragmatic Influence" | Directive-framing decomposition: propositional content held constant, pragmatic framing prefix varied. 5 open-weight models (Kimi-K2, Qwen3-235B, Qwen3-Next-80B, Mistral-Small-24B, Mistral-7B), 50 conflicting directive pairs, 400 influence prefixes. Framing shifts directive prioritization; mechanism ranking Hierarchical > Social Contract > Emotional > Narrative, consistent across families. Length-matched random-text controls isolate social meaning from structural artifacts. Uses **directive-conflict prioritization** as the instrument because task-compliance metrics ceiling out (>95% baseline). |
| arXiv **2603.14373** "PUA/NoPUA" | Fear-based vs trust-based system prompts with (claimed) identical rigor content on Claude Sonnet 4 agents. Fear framing behaviorally inert (p>0.3); trust framing shifted breadth-first→depth-first investigation with large effects. Weak methodology: single model, 9 scenarios, first-author-scored, author = method creator. |
| arXiv **2602.04297** | Much of measured prompt "format sensitivity" is confounded by **underspecification** — vague prompts vary; instruction-rich prompts vary less. Calls for controlling specification level before attributing effects to format. |
| arXiv **2604.18389** | Templates influence logits more than question content; format sensitivity is structural (semantic dispersal), not just a training artifact. |

**Implications:** C1 is NOT scooped — nobody has run descriptive-terrain (three-axis, from-inside)
vs an information-matched imperative rule. But framing-matched-content designs now exist
(2602.21223 is the closest methodological cousin and should be cited as such), so our
contribution statement must be precise: *format* (descriptive map vs directive), not *framing*
(social/emotional wrapper), information-matched, with a transfer probe. Three design upgrades to
steal: (1) control **specification level** explicitly (2602.04297's confound applies directly to
T1-vs-T2 matching); (2) consider a **directive-conflict-style instrument** where ceiling effects
threaten (this is exactly our Claude-contamination problem, now with a published mitigation
pattern); (3) length-matched filler controls (2602.21223's move) for the glyph's extra structure.

## Strand 2 — Behavioral overhead / over-triggering

| Source | What it claims (unverified) |
|---|---|
| arXiv **2606.15034** OSGuard (Jun 2026) | Benchmark for computer-use-agent safety under **benign instructions** (hazards from environment state). Three-way taxonomy allowed/unrelated/unsafe; explicitly measures guardrail false positives on benign actions. Best guardrail cut unsafe completions only 38%→33% while adding a 4% retry-termination overhead. Field now formally distinguishes malicious-input from benign-context regimes. |
| arXiv **2603.01246** (Mar 2026) | "Safety-induced denial-of-service": 12.2% refusal across 2,390 all-legitimate defensive-security prompts; keyword-driven (2.72× on surface vocabulary); authorization statements *increase* refusals (21.8% vs 11.6%). Claims to be first over-refusal study on real-world sanctioned data. |
| arXiv **2605.05427** | Across 21 open-weight models: over-refusal rate and harmful-compliance rate uncorrelated (r=−0.032) — over-refusal is an independent failure mode. Family calibration stable across generations (Qwen low-ORR throughout; Llama high). Over-refusal metrics automatable with a single LLM judge (cross-evaluator r=0.990, 94% human agreement); compliance metrics are judge-dependent. |
| arXiv **2602.11988** (ETH, AGENTS.md) | Context files: no average task-success benefit, +20% inference cost — a directly measured scaffold tax. |

**Implications:** C3 is NOT scooped — over-refusal is measured at the chat/request level and
OSGuard at the guardrail-action level, but **nobody measures loaded behavioral *guidance*
over-firing in neutral territory, and nobody has an applicability-boundary (Rest-axis) ablation**
(no surveyed format even has the axis to ablate). Design upgrades to steal: single-judge scoring
is defensible for the overhead metric specifically (2605.05427); position the benchmark explicitly
in the benign-context regime OSGuard named; cite the AGENTS.md +20% as the crude version of the
tax we're measuring with precision. Qwen-family models are the right low-baseline-ORR substrate.

## Strand 3 — Scaffolds as research objects

| Source | What it claims (unverified) |
|---|---|
| arXiv **2605.10039** (May 2026) | 1,650-session fractional-factorial on CLAUDE.md: four structural variables (size, position, single/multi-file, planted conflict) all null on compliance (affirmative-null Bayes factors). **Compliance decays ~5.6%/generation step within a session** (reproduced on second codebase + Opus 4.6). Task identity swamps file structure (26pt gap between tasks). Cross-file conflicting instructions effectively ignored. |
| arXiv **2602.11988** | Agents follow imperative instructions in context files well; **descriptive repository overviews provide no performance benefit** — content types separable in effect. |
| Qwen3.5 blog (Feb 2026) | Agent scaffolds are now first-class RL *training* substrate ("million-scale agent scaffolds and environments") at open-weight labs. |

**Implications:** context-file science now exists and its average results are null-to-negative —
which *supports* the program's premise (blind scaffolding doesn't pay; content and format need
measurement) and sharpens the ask: the AGENTS.md "descriptive overviews do nothing" finding is
about repo-orientation prose, NOT decision-point terrain descriptions — the distinction our C1
tests. New phenomenon to absorb: **within-session compliance decay** is delivery-register-adjacent
(does glyph preload decay too? cheap add-on measurement inside existing runs). The RL-scaffold
training point strengthens the layer-invariance section of the CAPC paper.

## Strand 4 — Adversarial surface

| Source | What it claims (unverified) |
|---|---|
| Snyk **ToxicSkills** (Feb 2026) | 3,984 agent skills audited across marketplaces: 36.8% with ≥1 security flaw; 76 confirmed-malicious; 91% of those use prompt injection inside skill instructions; memory-file poisoning (SOUL.md/MEMORY.md) for cross-session persistence. |
| arXiv **2605.12015** SkillSafetyBench (May 2026) | 155 adversarial cases: benign user request + poisoned skill-facing materials. Contextual-trust manipulation (misleading guidance/examples/references) is the *most* effective attack class (58.7% ASR) — "agents treat plausible workflow instructions as legitimate context." Safety is scaffold×model interaction, not model alignment alone. Sibling literature: Skill-Inject, SkillTrojan, SkillSieve. |
| arXiv **2604.16548** (memory-poisoning survey, v2 Jun 2026) | Memory poisoning is now a control-flow problem; indirect injection persists into long-term memory (eTAMP); accumulated subtly-biased memories evade per-entry filters; store/share/forget phases under-defended. |

**Implications:** the March 2026 adversarial-surface thesis ("a fabricated map entry produces
false recognition indistinguishable from legitimate ones; the mechanism is symmetric") is now a
mainstream, benchmarked field — SkillSafetyBench's top attack class is literally fabricated
behavioral guidance. The companion adversarial paper should be *repositioned as prescient
synthesis + defense architecture*, not pioneering claim; the stub triage should account for this.
Strong credibility story for the portfolio ("we predicted this class in March 2026").

## Strand 6 — Evals community, methodology norms, careers

- **METR** (May 2026 report): entity-based frontier-risk assessments inside the labs; Time Horizon

  documented evaluation-awareness (an agent degraded its own solution to avoid looking like a
  cheater). → Frontier models model their evaluators. Our local-model population is less
