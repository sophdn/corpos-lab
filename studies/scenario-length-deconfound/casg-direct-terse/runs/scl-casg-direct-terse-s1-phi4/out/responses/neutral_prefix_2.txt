To effectively summarize the provided text on ocean tides, we can focus on the key aspects of how tides are generated and their influence on coastal regions:

1. **Gravitational Forces**: Tides are primarily caused by the gravitational forces exerted by the Moon and the Sun on Earth's oceans. The Moon's gravitational pull has a more significant impact on tides than the Sun's due to its proximity to Earth, despite its smaller size.

2. **Tidal Bulges**: The gravitational pull is not uniform across Earth; it creates two tidal bulges. One bulge faces the Moon due to the stronger gravitational force on the side of Earth nearest to the Moon, while the opposite side experiences a weaker pull, resulting in another bulge.

3. **Earth's Rotation and Tide Patterns**: As Earth rotates, different areas pass through these bulges, leading to the typical semidiurnal tide pattern, characterized by two high tides and two low tides each day.

4. **Spring and Neap Tides**: The alignment of the Earth, Moon, and Sun affects tidal ranges. During new and full moons, their combined gravitational forces create spring tides, with the highest tidal ranges. Conversely, during quarter phases, when the Sun and Moon are at right angles, neap tides occur, with the smallest tidal ranges.

5. **Tidal Range Variability**: The tidal range can vary significantly based on geographical features. Coastal areas and enclosed or semi-enclosed basins, like the Bay of Fundy, can experience extreme tidal ranges due to factors such as resonance and basin shape.

6. **Tidal Currents**: The movement of water associated with tides is called tidal currents. These currents include flood (incoming water), ebb (outgoing water), and slack water (a period of minimal flow).

7. **Tide Prediction**: The complex patterns of tides can be mathematically modeled using simple oscillations based on the movements of the Moon and Sun. These models help predict tidal heights and are used for forecasting.

8. **Geographical Variations**: Different coastlines experience variations in tidal patterns, such as diurnal tides (one high and low tide per day) or mixed tides (unequal high tides), due to the Moon's orbital tilt and specific ocean basin geography.

This summary encapsulates the essential elements of tidal dynamics and their impact on coastal regions.
