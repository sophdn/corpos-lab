 1. Draft the Postmortem: Begin by outlining the key details of the outage, including its start and end times, the services affected, and the impact on users.

2. Follow the Template: Using the established template for postmortems in your organization, fill in the relevant sections such as the summary, timeline of events, root cause analysis, resolution, and follow-up actions.

3. Name the Document: Use the agreed naming convention for the postmortem document, which may include the date of the outage, the service affected, and a unique identifier.

4. Choose the Directory: Identify the directory where similar postmortem documents are stored and create the postmortem document in that location.

5. Identify Reviewers: Based on your org's postmortem governance protocol, assign the reviewers for the document. This is usually the team responsible for the service during the outage, as well as relevant leadership and stakeholders.

6. Send the Postmortem: Send the completed postmortem to the assigned reviewers for their feedback and approval.

7. Incorporate Feedback: Review and incorporate any feedback from the reviewers, updating the document as necessary.

8. Publish the Postmortem: Once approved, publish the postmortem to a central repository accessible to the team and other stakeholders, ensuring it is searchable and easily discoverable.

9. Communicate the Postmortem: Share the postmortem with the affected parties, including the users, and document any actions taken to address the issues uncovered during the outage.

10. Schedule Follow-up: Schedule a follow-up meeting to discuss the postmortem findings, review the actions taken, and ensure accountability for the implementation of those actions.
