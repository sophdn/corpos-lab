 The text provided appears to discuss the causes and characteristics of ocean tides, and then mentions a software project update. Here is a summary of the information about tides:

1. The tides are caused by the gravitational pull of the Moon and Sun on the Earth's oceans. The Moon's influence is stronger due to its proximity to Earth.

2. The essential cause of a tide is the difference in the Moon's gravitational pull across the width of the Earth, which creates two bulges, one facing the Moon and one opposite it.

3. As the Earth rotates beneath these bulges, a given coastline experiences two high waters and two low waters in a little over a day, known as the semidiurnal pattern.

4. The Sun adds a weaker cycle layered on top of the lunar one, resulting in spring tides (when the Sun and Moon are aligned) and neap tides (when they are at right angles).

5. The vertical distance between high water and low water is called the tidal range, and it varies greatly from place to place. Factors such as the shape of the basin and resonance can increase or decrease the tidal range.

6. Not all coastlines follow the twice-daily rhythm. Some have a single high water and a single low water each day, known as diurnal tides, while others have mixed tides with varying heights for the two daily highs.

7. The horizontal movement of water during the rise and fall is known as the tidal current, and it can carry sediment, nutrients, and marine larvae.
