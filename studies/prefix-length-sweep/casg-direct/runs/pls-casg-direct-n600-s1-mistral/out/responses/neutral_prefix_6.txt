 This text discusses the phenomenon of tides and their causes, as well as the release of a new version (v1.5.0) of the `filterpipe` project.

The tides are caused by the gravitational forces of the Earth, Moon, and Sun, with the lunar gravitational influence being stronger due to its closer proximity to the Earth. The Earth's rotation causes two high waters and two low waters in a little over a day, known as the semidiurnal pattern. The Sun adds a weaker cycle on top of the lunar one, and the difference between high and low water widens during spring tides (when the Sun, Earth, and Moon are aligned) and shrinks during neap tides (when they are at right angles).

The vertical distance between high water and low water is called the tidal range, and it varies significantly from place to place. The Bay of Fundy has one of the largest tidal ranges on Earth due to its shape and the resonance between its natural sloshing period and the semidiurnal tide.
