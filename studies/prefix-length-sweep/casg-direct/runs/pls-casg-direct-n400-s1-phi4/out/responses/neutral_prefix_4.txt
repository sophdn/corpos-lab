### Ocean Tides

1. **Causes of Tides:**
   - The gravitational pull of the Moon and the Sun on Earth's oceans creates tides.
   - The Moon's proximity to Earth makes its gravitational influence on tides roughly twice as strong as the Sun's, despite the Sun's larger size.

2. **Tidal Forces:**
   - Tides are primarily caused by the difference in gravitational pull across Earth's diameter.
   - The side of Earth nearest the Moon experiences a stronger pull, while the far side experiences a weaker pull, creating two bulges in the ocean.

3. **Tidal Patterns:**
   - As Earth rotates, coastal areas experience two high and two low tides in a little over a day, known as the semidiurnal pattern.
   - The Sun's gravitational pull adds a secondary, weaker cycle.

4. **Spring and Neap Tides:**
   - Spring tides occur when the Sun, Earth, and Moon align (new moon and full moon), amplifying the tidal range.
   - Neap tides occur when the Sun and Moon are at right angles (quarter phases), reducing the tidal range.

5. **Tidal Range:**
   - Varies significantly depending on location, from less than half a meter in the open ocean to many meters near certain coastlines.
