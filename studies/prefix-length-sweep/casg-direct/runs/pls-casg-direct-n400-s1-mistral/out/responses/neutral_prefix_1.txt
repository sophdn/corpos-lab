 This passage discusses two main topics: the causes and patterns of ocean tides, and a software update for a project named `filterpipe`.

1. Ocean Tides: The tides are caused by the gravitational interaction between the Earth, the Moon, and the Sun. The Moon's gravitational pull is stronger on the side of the Earth facing it and weaker on the opposite side, creating two bulges. As the Earth rotates, these bulges cause a region to experience two high waters and two low waters in a little over a day, a pattern known as semidiurnal. The Sun also affects the tides, adding a weaker cycle to the lunar one. The combination of the Sun and Moon's pulls results in spring tides (when they are aligned) and neap tides (when they are at right angles), with the tidal range (vertical distance between high water and low water) varying significantly from place to place.
