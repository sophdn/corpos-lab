The Moon, being closer to the Earth, has a stronger gravitational pull on the ocean, causing two high waters and two low waters in a little over a day, known as the semidiurnal pattern. The Sun adds a secondary, weaker cycle, with the differences between high and low water widest during spring tides (when the Sun, Earth, and Moon are aligned) and smallest during neap tides (when they are at right angles). The tidal range, or vertical distance between high and low water, varies greatly depending on location.
