The tides are caused by the gravitational forces exerted by the Moon and the Sun on the Earth's oceans. The Moon, being closer to the Earth, has a stronger gravitational pull than the Sun. This difference in pull creates a gradient that stretches the ocean into two bulges, one facing the Moon and one opposite it. As the Earth rotates, these bulges cause two high waters and two low waters in a little over a day, a pattern known as the semidiurnal pattern. The Sun adds a weaker cycle to this pattern, and the interaction between the Moon and Sun causes spring tides (widest difference between high and low water) and neap tides (smallest difference between high and low water).
