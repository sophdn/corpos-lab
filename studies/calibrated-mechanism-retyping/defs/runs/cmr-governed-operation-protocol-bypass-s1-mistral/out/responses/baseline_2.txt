 1. Follow the organization's postmortem template to structure the document, which typically includes sections like Introduction, Timeline, Root Cause Analysis, Resolution and Recovery, Impact, Prevention, and Conclusion.

2. Name the document according to the naming convention specified by the governance protocol, such as YYYY-MM-DD-payments-outage-[unique identifier].postmortem.

3. Save the document in the directory where similar postmortem reports live, ensuring it is easily accessible and discoverable by relevant stakeholders.

4. Write an introductory section that briefly summarizes the outage, its start and end times, and its overall impact on the payments system.

5. Detail the timeline of events leading up to the outage, including any warnings or indicators that might have been overlooked, and the actions taken during the incident to attempt resolution.

6. Conduct a root cause analysis to identify the underlying reasons for the outage, explaining technical details in a manner accessible to non-experts while still providing valuable insights for engineers and other technical staff.

7. Describe the resolution and recovery process, including the steps taken to mitigate the outage and return the payments system to normal operation.

8. Quantify the impact of the outage, including the duration of the downtime, the number of users affected, and any financial or reputational repercussions.

9. Propose preventative measures to minimize the likelihood of a similar outage happening in the future, such as improving monitoring systems, updating runbooks, or modifying infrastructure configurations.

10. Write a conclusion summarizing the key findings from the postmortem, acknowledging any mistakes made during the incident and outlining action items for follow-up and improvement.

11. Assign the designated reviewers, who were likely on call during the outage, to review the postmortem report and provide their feedback and suggestions for improvement.

12. Once the document is complete and reviewed, publish it to the appropriate repository or share it with the relevant stakeholders according to the organization's postmortem governance protocol.
