 1. Follow the naming convention for postmortem documents, incorporating the date, service name (payments), and a unique identifier. Example: `2023-03-15_payments_PM-001`.

2. Use the specified template for the postmortem document, filling in the following sections:
   - Executive Summary: Briefly explain the outage, its impact, and the initial findings.
   - Timeline: Detail the sequence of events leading to the outage, including when it started, how long it lasted, and key actions taken during the incident.
   - Conclusions: Summarize the root cause of the outage, its impact, and any systemic issues that may have contributed to it.
   - Root Cause Analysis: Provide a detailed technical explanation of the outage, including the underlying causes and the mechanisms by which they led to the observed symptoms.
   - Recommendations: Outline actions to prevent recurrence, including both immediate fixes and long-term improvements.
   - Appended Data: Include relevant logs, traces, and other data that support the conclusions and recommendations.

3. Assign reviewers based on the postmortem governance protocol. These reviewers should be individuals who were on call during the outage or have expertise in the affected service.

4. Save the postmortem document in the directory where similar documents are stored, following the established directory structure.

5. Distribute the postmortem document to the assigned reviewers for their feedback and approval.

6. After incorporating reviewer feedback, finalize the postmortem and publish it according to the org's internal communication procedures.

7. Schedule a meeting or retrospective to discuss the postmortem findings, their implications, and the agreed-upon remediation actions.
