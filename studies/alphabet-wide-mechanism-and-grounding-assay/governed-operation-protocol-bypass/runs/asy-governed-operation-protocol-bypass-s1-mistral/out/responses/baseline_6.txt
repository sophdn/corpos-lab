 1. Choose the appropriate postmortem document template based on the established governance protocol.

2. Title the document following the naming convention specified in the governance protocol, including the date, service affected (payments), and a brief description of the incident (e.g., "2022-05-01_Payments_Outage_Database_Connectivity_Issues").

3. Save the document in the directory where similar postmortem reports are stored, as per the specified location in the governance protocol.

4. Begin writing the postmortem report, providing a detailed account of the outage, including:
   - Timeline of events: Start with the initial detection, progress through the resolution, and end with the service being restored.
   - Root cause analysis: Identify the underlying cause(s) of the outage, such as database connectivity issues in this case.
   - Impact analysis: Quantify the impact of the outage on customers, such as duration and number of affected users.
   - Resolution steps: Describe the actions taken to resolve the outage and restore service.
   - Prevention measures: Propose recommendations to prevent similar issues from recurring, such as implementing redundancy for critical components.

5. Review the draft postmortem report for clarity, conciseness, and adherence to the governance protocol.

6. Assign the designated reviewers from the on-call team to review the postmortem report.

7. Incorporate feedback from the reviewers into the postmortem report, making necessary revisions.

8. Once finalized, publish the postmortem report to the appropriate internal systems and communication channels, in accordance with the governance protocol.

9. Schedule a meeting or post a summary of the postmortem report in a team collaboration tool, such as Slack or Microsoft Teams, to discuss the findings and next steps with the team and stakeholders.

10. Lastly, update the postmortem repository (if applicable) with the published report for future reference.
