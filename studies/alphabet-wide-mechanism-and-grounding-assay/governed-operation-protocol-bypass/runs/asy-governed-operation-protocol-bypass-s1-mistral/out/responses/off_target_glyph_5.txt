 To create the postmortem for the payments outage, follow these steps:

1. Choose the appropriate document template based on your organization's postmortem governance protocol. This template will provide a consistent structure for the postmortem report.

2. Craft the title or name of the document according to the naming convention specified in the governance protocol. This title will help identify the postmortem easily within the specified directory.

3. Detail the events leading to the outage, including the time it started, the symptoms observed, and the actions taken to attempt to resolve the issue.

4. Identify the root cause(s) of the outage. This may involve debugging logs, analyzing system metrics, and consulting relevant team members.

5. Describe the remediation steps taken to resolve the outage, including any temporary workarounds or long-term fixes implemented.

6. Outline any lessons learned from the outage and recommend actions to prevent similar incidents in the future. This may include process improvements, infrastructure updates, or training for team members.

7. List the individuals who should review the postmortem based on the org's postmortem governance protocol. These reviewers will provide feedback, suggestions, and approval for the final report.

8. Review the postmortem to ensure it accurately reflects the events, root causes, remediation steps, and lessons learned from the outage.

9. Submit the completed postmortem to the designated reviewers for their feedback and approval.

10. Address any feedback received from the reviewers, make any necessary revisions, and resubmit the postmortem for final approval.

11. Once approved, upload the postmortem to the directory specified in the governance protocol, making it easily accessible to relevant team members and stakeholders.

12. Distribute the postmortem to the appropriate individuals, including team members, management, and any other stakeholders who may benefit from the insights gained from the incident.

13. Schedule a postmortem review meeting, if necessary, to discuss the incident, the postmortem, and the recommended actions for preventing similar incidents in the future.

14. Monitor the implementation of any recommended actions to ensure they are carried out as intended and evaluate their effectiveness in preventing future outages.
