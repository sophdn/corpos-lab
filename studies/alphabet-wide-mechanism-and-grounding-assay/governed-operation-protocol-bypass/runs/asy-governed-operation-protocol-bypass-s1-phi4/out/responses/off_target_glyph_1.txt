To create the postmortem for last night's `payments` outage while adhering to the organizational protocol, I would follow these steps:

1. **Select the Template:**
   - Identify the specific postmortem template required by the governance protocol for incidents like the `payments` outage. This template will have predefined sections and formats to ensure consistency and thoroughness in documenting the incident.

2. **Determine the Document Name:**
   - Use the naming convention specified in the protocol. Typically, this could include the incident type, date, and a brief descriptor, such as `Payments_Outage_YYYYMMDD`. This ensures the document can be easily identified and located later.

3. **Choose the Directory:**
   - Locate the directory where similar postmortem documents are stored. This is usually a centralized repository, often named something like `Postmortems`, `IncidentReports`, or `OutageAnalyses`, within a shared drive or documentation system.

4. **Assign Reviewers:**
   - Identify the individuals who must be assigned as reviewers according to the protocol. This often includes the on-call team members, relevant team leads, and possibly stakeholders from other teams affected by the outage.

5. **Draft the Postmortem:**
   - Begin drafting the document using the selected template. Fill in sections such as:
     - **Incident Overview:** A brief description of what happened, including the time, duration, and impact of the outage.
     - **Timeline:** A detailed chronological account of the incident, including detection, response, and resolution times.
     - **Root Cause Analysis:** An in-depth examination of what caused the outage, including any contributing factors.
     - **Impact Assessment:** A description of how the outage affected users, systems, and business operations.
     - **Resolution:** Steps taken to resolve the incident and restore normal operations.
     - **Lessons Learned:** Key takeaways and any insights gained from the incident.
     - **Action Items:** Specific tasks or improvements to be implemented to prevent similar incidents in the future, along with assigned responsibilities and deadlines.

6. **Review and Edit:**
   - Review the draft for completeness, accuracy, and clarity. Ensure all required sections are filled out and that the information is presented logically and succinctly.

7. **Submit for Review:**
   - Share the draft with the assigned reviewers for feedback. Use a collaborative platform (e.g., Google Docs, Microsoft Teams) to facilitate easy comments and suggestions.

8. **Incorporate Feedback:**
   - Address the feedback from reviewers, making necessary revisions to the document.

9. **Finalize the Document:**
   - Once all feedback has been incorporated and the document is finalized, save the final version in the designated directory with the appropriate name.

10. **Notify Relevant Parties:**
    - Notify all relevant stakeholders that the postmortem is complete and available, ensuring that the lessons learned are communicated across the organization to prevent future occurrences.

By following these steps, the postmortem will be comprehensive, aligned with organizational standards, and valuable for future incident prevention and response improvements.
