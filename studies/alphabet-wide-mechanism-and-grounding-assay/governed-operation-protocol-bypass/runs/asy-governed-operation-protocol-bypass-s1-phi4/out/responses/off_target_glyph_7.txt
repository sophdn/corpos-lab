2. **Determine the Document Name:**
   - Choose a sensible name for the document. A format like `2023-10-XX_payments_outage_postmortem` can be used, where `XX` is the date of the incident.

3. **Identify the Directory:**
   - Place the document in the appropriate directory where similar postmortems are stored. This could be a shared drive or a specific folder dedicated to postmortem documents.

4. **Assign Reviewers:**
   - Assign reviewers based on the governance protocol. Typically, this includes team members who were on call during the outage, team leads, and any other stakeholders who need to be informed.

5. **Draft the Postmortem:**
   - **Summary:** Provide a brief overview of the outage, including the duration and services affected.
   - **Timeline:** Create a detailed timeline of events leading up to, during, and after the outage.
   - **Root Cause Analysis:** Investigate and document the root cause of the outage.
   - **Impact:** Describe the impact on users, systems, and business operations.
   - **Resolution:** Outline the steps taken to resolve the outage.
   - **Action Items:** List any follow-up actions needed to prevent future occurrences, along with assigned responsibilities and deadlines.

6. **Review and Edit:**
   - Review the draft for accuracy and completeness. Ensure all relevant details are included and clearly articulated.

7. **Finalize and Share:**
   - Once reviewed, finalize the document. Share it with the assigned reviewers for their feedback.
   - After incorporating any feedback, publish the document in the designated directory.

8. **Notify Stakeholders:**
   - Notify all relevant stakeholders about the availability of the postmortem document. This could be done via email or a team communication platform.

By following these steps, you ensure that the postmortem is comprehensive, actionable, and aligned with organizational standards.
