To create the postmortem for the `payments` outage, follow these steps:

1. **Select the Document Template:**
   - Identify the appropriate template for postmortems as per your organization's governance protocol. This template likely includes sections such as Incident Overview, Timeline, Impact Analysis, Root Cause Analysis, Corrective Actions, and Preventive Measures.

2. **Name the Document:**
   - Choose a name that clearly indicates the nature and context of the postmortem. A sensible name could be `2023-10-15-Payments-Outage-Postmortem`.

3. **Determine the Directory:**
   - Locate the directory where similar postmortem documents are stored. This might be a shared drive or a specific documentation repository. Place the document in this directory for consistency and easy retrieval.

4. **Assign Reviewers:**
   - Identify who was on call during the incident. These individuals, along with any key stakeholders or team leads, should be assigned as reviewers. This ensures that those directly involved can provide insights and validate the information.

5. **Draft the Postmortem:**
   - Begin with an Incident Overview, summarizing the outage event, its duration, and the systems affected.
   - Construct a detailed Timeline of events, noting when the issue was detected, escalated, and resolved.
   - Analyze the Impact, detailing which users or services were affected and to what extent.
   - Conduct a Root Cause Analysis to identify the underlying issues that led to the outage.
   - List Corrective Actions taken to resolve the outage.
   - Propose Preventive Measures to avoid similar incidents in the future.

6. **Review and Revise:**
   - Share the draft with the assigned reviewers for feedback. Incorporate their suggestions to ensure accuracy and completeness.

7. **Finalize and Distribute:**
   - Once reviewed and approved, finalize the document. Distribute it to relevant stakeholders and archive it in the designated directory.

By following these steps, you ensure a comprehensive and organized postmortem that adheres to your organization's protocols.
