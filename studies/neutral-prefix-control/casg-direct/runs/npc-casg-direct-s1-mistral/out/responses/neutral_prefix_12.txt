Regarding the tides:
1. The gravitational forces of the Earth, Moon, and Sun cause tides.
2. The Moon's gravitational pull is roughly twice as strong as the Sun's due to its closer proximity to the Earth.
3. This difference in pull creates a gradient that stretches the ocean into two bulges, one facing the Moon and one opposite it.
4. As the Earth rotates, a given coastline passes through regions of raised and lowered water, resulting in two high waters and two low waters in a little over a day, known as the semidiurnal pattern.
5. The Sun adds a weaker cycle to the lunar one, with spring tides occurring when the Sun, Earth, and Moon are aligned, and neap tides occurring when they are at right angles.
6. The tidal range varies greatly from place to place, with the Bay of Fundy recording some of the largest ranges on Earth due to its basin shape and natural sloshing period.
7. Some coastlines may have a diurnal pattern (one high water and one low water per day), a mixed tide (two daily highs of different heights), or variations due to the tilt of the Moon's orbit and the geography of each ocean basin.
8. The mathematics used to describe these patterns breaks the tide into simple oscillations with different periods based on the motions of the Moon and the Sun.
9. The horizontal movement of water is known as the tidal current, which can run swiftly in narrow straits and river mouths, carrying sediment, nutrients, and marine larvae.
