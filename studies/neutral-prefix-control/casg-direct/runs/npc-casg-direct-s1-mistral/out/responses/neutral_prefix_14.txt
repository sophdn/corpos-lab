1. Ocean Tides Explanation:
   - Tides are primarily caused by the gravitational pull of the Moon and Sun on the Earth's oceans.
   - The lunar rhythm dominates the pattern of the tides across most of the world's coastlines because the Moon's gravitational influence on the ocean is roughly twice as strong as the Sun's.
   - The Earth rotates beneath the bulges created by the gravitational forces, resulting in two high waters and two low waters in a little over a day, known as the semidiurnal pattern.
   - The Sun adds a second, weaker cycle layered on top of the lunar one, causing spring and neap tides.
   - The tidal range, or the vertical distance between high water and low water, varies greatly from place to place, with some areas experiencing tides that exceed 15 meters.
   - The shape of the basin holding the water, and the resonance between the basin's natural sloshing period and the tide's rhythm, can greatly amplify tidal ranges.
   - Differences in the tilt of the Moon's orbit and the geography of each ocean basin can cause variations in the tidal pattern, resulting in diurnal, mixed, and other tide types.
   - The horizontal movement of water that accompanies the rise and fall is called the tidal current, and it can significantly affect the ecology of estuaries and mudflats.
