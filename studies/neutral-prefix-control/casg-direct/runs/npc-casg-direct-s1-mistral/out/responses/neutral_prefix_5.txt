 The provided text discusses the mechanisms of ocean tides and their variations, as well as an update about a software project named `filterpipe`.

Regarding the tides, the text explains that their primary cause is the difference in gravitational pull between the Earth, Moon, and Sun, which creates two bulges on the ocean facing the Moon and its opposite. As the Earth rotates, the water level rises and falls twice a day, creating a semidiurnal pattern of high and low tides. The Sun also affects the tides, causing spring tides when it aligns with the Earth and Moon, and neap tides when it is at right angles to them. The tidal range, or the vertical distance between high and low water, varies greatly depending on the geography of the coastline and the shape of the ocean basin.

The Bay of Fundy on the eastern edge of North America records some of the largest tidal ranges on Earth, reaching over fifteen meters during the strongest spring tides. This extreme is attributed to the natural sloshing period of the basin, which matches the period of the semidiurnal tide.
