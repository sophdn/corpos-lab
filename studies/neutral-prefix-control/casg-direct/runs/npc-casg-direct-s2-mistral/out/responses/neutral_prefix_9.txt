 In this text, the author explains the cause and patterns of tides in the ocean. The tides are primarily caused by the gravitational pull of the Moon and Sun on the Earth's oceans. The Moon's gravitational pull is stronger than the Sun's because it is closer to the Earth, causing the ocean to bulge on both sides of the Earth. As the Earth rotates beneath these bulges, coastal areas experience two high waters and two low waters in a little over a day, a pattern known as the semidiurnal pattern. The Sun also adds a weaker cycle to this pattern. The difference between high and low water is called the tidal range, and it varies significantly from place to place. The Bay of Fundy in North America records some of the largest tidal ranges on Earth, exceeding fifteen meters during the strongest spring tides.
