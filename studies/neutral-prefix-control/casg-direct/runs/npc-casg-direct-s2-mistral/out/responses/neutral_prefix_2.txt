The tides are caused by the gravitational forces of the Moon and Sun, creating bulges in the ocean as the Earth rotates. These bulges cause two high waters and two low waters in most shorelines in a pattern known as the semidiurnal pattern. The Sun adds a weaker cycle to this pattern, creating spring and neap tides. The tidal range varies greatly depending on factors such as the shape of the basin and resonance. Coastal areas experience tidal currents, which influence the ecology of estuaries and mudflats.
