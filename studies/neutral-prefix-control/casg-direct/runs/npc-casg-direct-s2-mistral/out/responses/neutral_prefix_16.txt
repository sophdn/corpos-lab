 In the given text, the focus is on the explanation of ocean tides, their causes, and their variations. The main points are:

1. Tides are caused by the gravitational forces of the Moon and the Sun on the Earth's oceans. The Moon's influence is stronger due to its closer proximity.

2. The Earth rotates beneath these gravitational forces, creating two bulges of water - one facing the Moon and one opposite to it.

3. As the Earth rotates, a given stretch of coastline experiences two high waters and two low waters in a little over a day, known as the semidiurnal pattern.

4. The Sun adds a second, weaker cycle to the lunar one, causing spring tides (widest difference between high and low water) during new moon and full moon, and neap tides (smallest difference between high and low water) during quarter phases.

5. The vertical distance between high water and low water is called the tidal range, which varies greatly from place to place.

6. The Bay of Fundy records some of the largest tidal ranges on Earth due to its unique shape and the resonance between its natural sloshing period and the tide's semidiurnal period.

7. Some coastlines may follow a single high water and low water each day (diurnal pattern), or a mixed pattern where two daily highs differ significantly.

8. The horizontal movement of water during the rise and fall is known as the tidal current, which can have significant ecological impacts on estuaries and mudflats.
