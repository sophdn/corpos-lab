 This text discusses the phenomenon of ocean tides and how they are influenced by the gravitational forces of the Earth, Moon, and Sun. The tides are primarily caused by the difference in gravitational pull across the width of the Earth, leading to two bulges of water, one facing the Moon and one opposite it. The Earth's rotation beneath these bulges results in two high waters and two low waters in a little over a day, known as the semidiurnal pattern. The Sun adds a weaker cycle to this pattern, with the difference between high and low water reaching its widest during spring tides and its smallest during neap tides. The tidal range, or vertical distance between high water and low water, varies greatly depending on location.
