Regarding the ocean tides: The tides are primarily caused by the gravitational pull of the Moon on Earth's oceans, with the Sun contributing a secondary influence. The difference in the Moon's pull across the width of the Earth creates two bulges, one facing the Moon and one opposite it. As the Earth rotates, a coastline experiences two high waters and two low waters in a little over a day, a rhythm known as the semidiurnal pattern. The Sun adds a second, weaker cycle, causing spring tides when aligned with the Moon and neap tides when at right angles. The tidal range (vertical difference between high water and low water) varies greatly from place to place, with the Bay of Fundy recording some of the largest ranges on Earth. Factors such as the shape of the basin and resonance can amplify the tides.

Regarding the `gridcache` software release: Version 2.3.0 has been cut, which includes the addition of a `TTLCache` class that expires entries after a configurable interval and a fix for `LRUCache.pop` returning a stale value after a concurrent eviction.
