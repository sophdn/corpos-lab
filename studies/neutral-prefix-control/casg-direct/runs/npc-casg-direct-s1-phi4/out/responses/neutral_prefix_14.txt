### Causes of Tides

1. **Gravitational Pull**: Tides are primarily caused by the gravitational forces exerted by the Moon and the Sun on the Earth's oceans. Despite the Sun's larger mass, the Moon's proximity to Earth makes its gravitational influence on the tides about twice as strong as that of the Sun.

2. **Tidal Bulges**: The Earth experiences two tidal bulges due to the differential gravitational pull. One bulge forms on the side of the Earth facing the Moon, where the gravitational pull is strongest, and the other on the opposite side, where the pull is weakest. This creates two high tides and two low tides each day, known as the semidiurnal pattern.

3. **Earth's Rotation**: As the Earth rotates, any given point on its surface moves through these bulges, experiencing cycles of rising and falling water levels.

4. **Solar Influence**: The Sun contributes a secondary cycle on top of the lunar one. During new and full moons, when the Earth, Moon, and Sun align, the solar and lunar tides reinforce each other, leading to spring tides with the highest tidal ranges. Conversely, during quarter moons, when the Sun and Moon are at right angles, their effects partially cancel out, resulting in neap tides with the smallest tidal ranges.

### Factors Affecting Tidal Range

1. **Tidal Range**: The vertical difference between high and low tides, known as the tidal range, varies significantly across different locations. It can be minimal in the open ocean and much larger near certain coastlines.

2. **Geographical Influence**: The shape of the coastline and the ocean basins can amplify tidal ranges. For example, the Bay of Fundy experiences some of the highest tidal ranges due to its natural sloshing period aligning with the semidiurnal tide period, a phenomenon known as resonance.

3. **Resonance**: When the natural period of an enclosed or partially enclosed body of water matches the tidal cycle, the tidal range can increase significantly.

4. **Tidal Patterns**: Different coastlines experience varying tidal patterns:
   - **Semidiurnal**: Two high and two low tides each day.
   - **Diurnal**: One high and one low tide each day.
   - **Mixed Tide**: Two high and two low tides each day, with one high tide significantly higher than the other.

### Additional Considerations

- **Tidal Currents**: The horizontal movement of water associated with tides is known as tidal currents. These include flood (incoming water) and ebb (outgoing water) currents, with slack water occurring between them.
- **Ecological Impact**: Tidal currents play a crucial role in estuarine and coastal ecosystems by transporting sediments, nutrients, and marine life, supporting diverse communities adapted to these conditions.

This comprehensive understanding of tides highlights the complex interplay between gravitational forces, Earth's rotation, and geographical features, all of which contribute to the diverse tidal phenomena observed around the world.
