### Ocean Tides

1. **Cause of Tides:**
   - Tides result from gravitational forces exerted by the Moon and the Sun on Earth's oceans.
   - The Moon's proximity makes its gravitational influence on tides roughly twice as strong as the Sun's, despite the Sun's larger size.
   - Tides are primarily caused by the differential gravitational pull across Earth's diameter, creating two bulges of water—one facing the Moon and one on the opposite side.

2. **Tidal Patterns:**
   - Most coastlines experience a semidiurnal pattern with two high tides and two low tides each day.
   - The Sun contributes a secondary, weaker tidal cycle.
   - **Spring Tides:** Occur during new and full moons when the Sun and Moon align, amplifying tidal ranges.
   - **Neap Tides:** Occur during quarter phases when the Sun and Moon are at right angles, reducing tidal ranges.

3. **Tidal Range:**
   - Varies significantly depending on location, with factors such as the shape of ocean basins and resonance playing a role.
   - The Bay of Fundy is noted for its extreme tidal ranges due to its natural period aligning with the semidiurnal tide.

4. **Variations:**
   - Some regions experience diurnal tides (one high and one low tide per day) or mixed tides (two highs/low tides of differing heights).
   - These variations are influenced by the Moon's orbit tilt and local geography.

5. **Tidal Currents:**
   - Tidal movements include flood (inward flow), ebb (outward flow), and slack water (period of near stillness).
   - Tidal currents affect sediment transport and marine ecology, particularly in estuaries and mudflats.
