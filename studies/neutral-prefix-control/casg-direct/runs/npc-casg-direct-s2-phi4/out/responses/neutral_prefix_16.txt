### Ocean Tides

1. **Gravitational Influence**: The tides are primarily caused by the gravitational pull of the Moon and the Sun on Earth's oceans. The Moon, despite being smaller than the Sun, exerts a stronger gravitational force on Earth's waters due to its closer proximity.

2. **Tidal Bulges**: The differential gravitational pull creates two tidal bulges on opposite sides of Earth. As Earth rotates, these bulges cause the cyclical rise and fall of sea levels known as tides.

3. **Tidal Patterns**: Most coastlines experience a semidiurnal pattern, with two high and two low tides each day. The Sun's gravitational pull adds a secondary, weaker cycle to the lunar one.

4. **Spring and Neap Tides**: Spring tides occur when the Sun, Earth, and Moon align, leading to higher high tides and lower low tides. Neap tides occur when the Sun and Moon are at right angles, resulting in less pronounced tidal differences.

5. **Tidal Range Variability**: The vertical distance between high and low tides, known as the tidal range, varies widely. Factors such as the shape of coastlines and ocean basins, as well as resonance, can amplify tidal ranges.

6. **Tidal Currents**: The horizontal movement of water during tidal changes is called tidal currents, which play a crucial role in shaping coastal ecosystems by transporting nutrients and organisms.
