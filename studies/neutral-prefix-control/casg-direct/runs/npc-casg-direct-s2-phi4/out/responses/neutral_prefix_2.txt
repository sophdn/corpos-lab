The passage describes the phenomenon of ocean tides, which are primarily caused by the gravitational interactions between the Earth, Moon, and Sun. Here's a breakdown of the key points:

1. **Gravitational Influence**: The tides are not caused by the gravitational pull of the Moon on the ocean alone but by the differential gravitational force across the Earth's diameter. The Moon's gravity pulls more strongly on the side of the Earth closest to it and less strongly on the side farthest from it, creating two tidal bulges.

2. **Rotation and Tidal Patterns**: As the Earth rotates, these tidal bulges move around the planet, causing most coastlines to experience two high tides and two low tides each day, known as the semidiurnal pattern.

3. **Solar Influence**: The Sun also affects tides, though its influence is weaker than the Moon's. When the Earth, Moon, and Sun align (during new and full moons), their combined gravitational pull causes spring tides, characterized by higher high tides and lower low tides. When the Sun and Moon are at right angles (during quarter moons), their gravitational forces partially cancel out, resulting in neap tides, which have the smallest tidal range.

4. **Tidal Range Variability**: The tidal range varies significantly across different locations. Factors influencing this include the shape of the ocean basin and resonance effects. For instance, the Bay of Fundy has one of the largest tidal ranges due to its natural sloshing period matching the tidal cycle.

5. **Tidal Currents**: The movement of water associated with tides is called tidal currents. These include the flood (inward flow during rising tide) and the ebb (outward flow during falling tide), with a brief period of slack water in between. These currents are crucial for the ecology of estuaries and mudflats.

6. **Coastal Variations**: Different coastal areas experience different tidal patterns, such as diurnal tides (one high and one low tide per day) or mixed tides (unequal daily high tides), influenced by the Moon's orbital tilt and the geography of ocean basins.
