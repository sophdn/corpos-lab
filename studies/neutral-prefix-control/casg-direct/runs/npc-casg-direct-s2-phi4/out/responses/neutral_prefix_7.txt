### Ocean Tides

1. **Gravitational Influence**: The tides are primarily caused by the gravitational pull of the Moon and, to a lesser extent, the Sun. The Moon's proximity to Earth gives it a stronger influence on ocean tides compared to the Sun.

2. **Tidal Bulges**: The gravitational pull causes the ocean to form two bulges: one facing the Moon and one on the opposite side. As Earth rotates, these bulges create high and low tides.

3. **Tidal Patterns**: 
   - **Semidiurnal Tides**: Most coastlines experience two high and two low tides each day.
   - **Spring Tides**: Occur during new and full moons when the Sun and Moon's gravitational forces align, resulting in higher high tides and lower low tides.
   - **Neap Tides**: Occur during quarter moons when the gravitational forces of the Sun and Moon are perpendicular, resulting in less extreme tides.

4. **Tidal Range**: The difference between high and low tides varies by location. Factors like the shape of the coastline and ocean basins can amplify tides through resonance.

5. **Tidal Currents**: The horizontal movement of water during tides includes flood (incoming) and ebb (outgoing) currents, with slack water occurring at the turning points.
