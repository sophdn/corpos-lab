### Ocean Tides

The passage describes the phenomenon of ocean tides, which are primarily caused by the gravitational forces exerted by the Moon and the Sun on the Earth's oceans. Here are the key points:

1. **Gravitational Forces**: The Moon, despite being much smaller than the Sun, has a stronger gravitational influence on the Earth's tides due to its proximity. This results in the lunar rhythm dominating tidal patterns globally.

2. **Tidal Bulges**: The gravitational pull of the Moon is not uniform across the Earth, creating a gradient. This results in two tidal bulges on opposite sides of the Earth, leading to high and low tides as the Earth rotates.

3. **Tidal Patterns**: Most coastlines experience a semidiurnal pattern, with two high and two low tides each day. The tidal range can vary significantly depending on geographical factors and the natural period of the basin.

4. **Spring and Neap Tides**: These are influenced by the alignment of the Sun, Earth, and Moon. Spring tides occur when the Sun and Moon align, enhancing the tidal range, while neap tides occur when they are at right angles, reducing the tidal range.

5. **Tidal Currents**: The movement of water associated with tides is known as the tidal current, which includes flood, ebb, and slack water phases. These currents play a crucial role in coastal ecology.
