### Ocean Tides

1. **Gravitational Influence**: Tides are caused by the gravitational pull of the Moon and the Sun on the Earth's oceans. The Moon, despite being smaller, has a stronger influence on tides due to its proximity to Earth.

2. **Tidal Bulges**: The gravitational pull creates two tidal bulges on opposite sides of the Earth. As the Earth rotates, these bulges cause high and low tides at various coastlines.

3. **Tidal Patterns**:
   - **Semidiurnal Tides**: Most coastlines experience two high and two low tides each day.
   - **Spring Tides**: Occur when the Sun, Earth, and Moon are aligned, resulting in higher high tides and lower low tides.
   - **Neap Tides**: Occur when the Sun and Moon are at right angles, leading to less extreme tides.

4. **Tidal Range**: The difference between high and low water levels varies by location. Factors like the shape of the coastline and natural resonances can amplify tidal ranges.

5. **Tidal Currents**: The horizontal movement of water during rising and falling tides, affecting sediment transport and marine ecology.
