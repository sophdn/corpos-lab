The ocean tides are primarily driven by the gravitational pull of the Moon and, to a lesser extent, the Sun. The Moon's gravitational pull creates two tidal bulges on Earth: one on the side facing the Moon and another on the opposite side. As Earth rotates, these bulges cause high and low tides, generally resulting in two high tides and two low tides each day, known as the semidiurnal pattern.

The Sun also influences tides, adding a weaker cycle on top of the lunar cycle. When the Earth, Moon, and Sun align (during new and full moons), the solar and lunar gravitational forces reinforce each other, leading to spring tides with the largest tidal range. Conversely, during the first and third quarter phases of the Moon, when the Sun and Moon are at right angles, their gravitational forces partially cancel out, resulting in neap tides with the smallest tidal range.

The tidal range varies significantly depending on geographical factors. For example, the Bay of Fundy experiences some of the highest tidal ranges on Earth due to its natural resonant period that matches the semidiurnal tidal rhythm. This resonance amplifies the tidal range. Coastal features, such as long inlets or basins with specific natural periods, can also amplify tides through funneling effects and resonance.

In addition to vertical movements, tides involve horizontal water movement known as tidal currents. These currents include the flood (inward flow as the tide rises), the ebb (outward flow as the tide falls), and slack water (a brief period of stillness between flood and ebb). Tidal currents are crucial for transporting sediment, nutrients, and marine life, significantly impacting coastal and estuarine ecosystems.

The complexity of tidal patterns arises from the interplay of the Moon's and Sun's gravitational forces, Earth's rotation, and the unique geography of each ocean basin. Coastal authorities use mathematical models to forecast tides by breaking them down into a combination of simple oscillations with periods linked to the motions of the Moon and Sun.
