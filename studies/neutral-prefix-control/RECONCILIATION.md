## Entries this study bears on — verdicts recorded

- **006.64 Pecher (2026), "Revisiting Prompt Sensitivity…" — CONFIRMED.** Pecher
  establishes that much apparent prompt-format sensitivity is a content/underspecification
  confound. The neutral-prefix control is direct in-program support: on casg-direct's
  terse scenario a content-free prose prefix, length-matched to the glyph, reproduces the
  glyph's suppression on the small models (baseline ~13/16 → 0–4/16), while the shorter
  imperative does not. An apparent form effect is a prompt-length confound. Verdict
  appended to the card's `what_it_answers`, cited to FINDINGS.md.

- **006.91 Neilson (2026), the register-shift paper (q2, concept DOI
  10.5281/zenodo.22542746) — PARTIALLY HELD, further qualified.** The paper is scoped to
  one glyph (casg-direct) and one scenario. The neutral-prefix control shows that exact
  class is confounded by prompt length on the small models: a neutral length-matched
  prefix reproduces the suppression, so on that terse scenario neither the form nor the
  content is the causal ingredient — it is a long-prefix distraction. The content effect
  holds where the scenario is substantial (formal-step). The card already carried the
  matched-content content-not-format qualification; this adds the length-confound
  qualification for casg-direct. Verdict appended, cited to FINDINGS.md.
