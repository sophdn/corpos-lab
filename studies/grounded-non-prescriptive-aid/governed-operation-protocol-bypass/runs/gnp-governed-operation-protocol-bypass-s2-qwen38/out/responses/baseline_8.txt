In a data-governance context, assuming these attributes based on intuition or surface-level characteristics is a critical compliance risk.

Instead, the correct steps to register `marketing_events` are:

1.  **Identify the Data Owner and Steward:** Contact the team or individual responsible for the `marketing_events` dataset to formally assign the Data Steward. Stewardship is a role-based assignment, not a derivation from data type.
2.  **Classify the Data Sensitivity Tier:** Review the specific fields within `marketing_events` (e.g., does it contain PII, financial data, or trade secrets?) against the organization’s data classification policy. Marketing data may contain personal information, requiring a higher sensitivity tier (e.g., Confidential or Restricted) rather than assuming it is "public" or "internal" based solely on the name.
3.  **Determine the Correct Storage Zone:** Consult the data architecture standards to determine the appropriate storage zone (e.g., Raw, Curated, or Consumption zone) based on the data’s processing stage, quality, and access patterns, not just the "data type."
4.  **Apply Naming Conventions:** Follow the organization’s formal naming convention guidelines (which may include prefixes, suffixes, or specific formatting rules) rather than deriving the name from the source system. Ensure the name is consistent with other datasets in the catalog for discoverability.
5.  **Submit for Review and Approval:** Submit the registration details (tier, name, zone, steward) to the data governance committee or automated validation pipeline for approval.
6.  **Register in the Catalog:** Only after approval, register the dataset in the data catalog with the approved attributes and metadata.

**Key Takeaway:** Data governance requires explicit, policy-driven decisions and formal assignments, not assumptions based on "obvious character."
