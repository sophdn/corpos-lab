 1. Consult the Postmortem Governance Protocol to determine the prescribed document template, naming convention, directory, and reviewer list.

2. Based on the protocol, select the appropriate document template for the postmortem. This could be a pre-defined template provided by the organization, or a template specific to the `payments` service.

3. Apply the naming convention defined in the protocol to the postmortem title. This convention might include information about the service, the type of outage, and the date. An example could be: "2023-03-15 Payments Service Outage Postmortem."

4. Determine the appropriate directory for the postmortem based on the protocol. This could be a shared folder for all postmortems, a sub-folder for each service, or a specific folder designated for critical incidents.

5. Identify the reviewers according to the reviewer list provided in the protocol. This could involve consulting a list of key personnel, on-call rotation schedules, or specific roles within the organization.

6. Begin writing the postmortem using the selected template. Include details about the outage, the root cause, the timeline, actions taken to resolve the issue, and any follow-up actions to prevent similar outages in the future.

7. After the initial draft is complete, share it with the assigned reviewers for their feedback and approval.

8. Make any necessary revisions based on the feedback received from the reviewers.

9. Once finalized, publish the postmortem in the designated directory according to the protocol.

10. Notify relevant parties about the availability of the postmortem, including team members, management, and stakeholders.
