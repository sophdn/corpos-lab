 1. Choose the appropriate postmortem document template based on the organizational standard.

2. Draft the title of the postmortem. It should include the service affected (payments), the date and time of the outage, and a brief summary of the incident (e.g., "Payments Service Outage on [Date and Time]").

3. Populate the postmortem document with the following sections:
   - Summary: Brief overview of the outage, its impact, and the main findings.
   - Timeline: Detailed chronology of the incident, including the onset, escalation, and resolution.
   - Conclusions: Analysis of the root causes, contributing factors, and any systemic issues discovered.
   - Recommendations: Proposed actions to prevent similar incidents in the future, including immediate remedies and long-term improvements.
   - Action Items: Assign specific action items to team members responsible for implementation.
   - Appendices: Include any relevant logs, error messages, or screenshots that support the analysis and conclusions.

4. Review the document to ensure it is clear, concise, and adheres to the organizational postmortem governance protocol.

5. Identify the reviewers based on the on-call rotation. These reviewers should provide feedback and suggestions to improve the postmortem.

6. Send the postmortem document to the reviewers for their feedback.

7. Incorporate the feedback from the reviewers into the postmortem document.

8. Once finalized, save the postmortem in the designated directory where similar documents live, following the naming convention established by your organization.

9. Notify the team and relevant stakeholders of the completion of the postmortem and provide them with a link or attachment to the document.

10. Schedule a retrospective meeting with the team to discuss the postmortem findings, address any open issues, and ensure that the action items are being addressed.
