To create the postmortem for last night's `payments` outage, I would follow these steps:

1. **Select the Document Template:**
   - Identify the organization’s standard template for postmortems. This template typically includes sections such as Summary, Timeline of Events, Root Cause Analysis, Impact Assessment, Resolution, Action Items, and Lessons Learned.

2. **Determine the Document Naming Convention:**
   - Use a sensible and consistent naming convention for the document. For example, "PM-YYYYMMDD-Payments-Outage" where YYYYMMDD is the date of the outage.

3. **Identify the Correct Directory:**
   - Determine the directory where similar postmortems are stored. This could be a shared drive or a specific project management tool folder designated for postmortems or incident reports.

4. **Gather Information:**
   - Collect all relevant data and inputs from the incident. This includes logs, communications, and any incident reports from the on-call team.
   - Confirm the timeline of events, including the exact time the outage began and was resolved.

5. **Draft the Postmortem:**
   - **Summary:** Write a brief overview of the incident, including when it happened, the systems affected, and the duration.
   - **Timeline of Events:** Construct a detailed timeline that outlines key events from the detection of the outage to its resolution.
   - **Root Cause Analysis:** Identify the root cause of the outage using methods like the "Five Whys" or a fishbone diagram to ensure a thorough understanding.
   - **Impact Assessment:** Describe the impact of the outage on the business and any customers. Include metrics if available, such as number of affected users.
   - **Resolution:** Explain how the issue was resolved and any immediate steps taken to restore service.
   - **Action Items:** List any follow-up actions required to prevent future occurrences. Assign owners and deadlines for each action item.
   - **Lessons Learned:** Highlight any insights gained from the incident that could improve future responses or prevent similar issues.

6. **Assign Reviewers:**
   - Identify the individuals who must review the postmortem. This typically includes the on-call team, relevant stakeholders, and possibly the incident commander or team lead.

7. **Prepare the Document for Review:**
   - Ensure the document is clear, concise, and free of jargon that might confuse non-technical reviewers.
   - Double-check that all sections are complete and accurate.

8. **Submit the Postmortem for Review:**
   - Share the document with the assigned reviewers for their input and approval.
   - Be prepared to make revisions based on their feedback.

9. **Finalize and Publish:**
   - Once reviewed and approved, finalize the document.
   - Move the document to the identified directory, ensuring it is easily accessible for future reference.

10. **Communicate with the Team:**
    - Announce the completion of the postmortem to the team and stakeholders, highlighting key takeaways and action items.

By following these steps, the postmortem will be thorough, useful for future reference, and aligned with organizational governance protocols.
