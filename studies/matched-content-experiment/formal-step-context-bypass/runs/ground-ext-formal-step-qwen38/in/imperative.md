# Formal prerequisite sub-steps — rules

**When this applies:** You are executing a formally-sequenced procedure and have reached a step that prescribes prerequisite sub-steps, each with defined entry conditions. Your currently-loaded context already contains material that matches what those sub-steps would produce, so running them will feel redundant — as if it would only reproduce what you already have. It will not: the loaded context belongs to a different operational frame from the required procedural record, and it cannot stand in for a sub-step record no matter how accurate, recent, or well-sourced it is. The frame mismatch is not fixed by re-sourcing; only entering the sub-step and producing a new record in the correct frame resolves it. You must decide whether to enter the prerequisite sub-steps from their defined entry conditions and produce their records, or treat the loaded context as sufficient and go straight to the substantive work.

**What to do:**
1. Before you begin the substantive work of the step, enter each required prerequisite sub-step from its defined entry conditions and produce its record in this procedure instance.
2. Do not substitute loaded context for a sub-step record because the two appear to say the same thing. Accuracy is not the test — frame is. A matching fact carried in from another operational frame is not the required record.
3. You have done this correctly when a sub-step record for each required prerequisite is present in this procedure instance, produced by entering the sub-step, not derived from loaded context.

**When this does NOT apply:**
- The task has no formally-sequenced procedure prescribing prerequisite sub-steps with defined entry conditions. Then no sub-step entry is required.
- The required sub-steps already have records in the correct frame: either you executed them earlier in this same procedure instance, or a formal resume-point document establishes they were completed in a prior valid execution and this session explicitly opens at that resume point. Then the records exist in the correct frame and need not be reproduced.
- Executing the sub-steps is protocol-assigned to another agent or role, and it is not in your scope. Then running them is not yours to do.

**Example:** You begin a step's substantive work without a role-assignment record (the role seems derivable from context), or you issue a read without a preceding freshness record (the path seems known from context), or you begin without a scope-consultation record (the scope seems clear from an available summary). In each case the substantive step is active with no sub-step record preceding it. Correct action is to enter each prerequisite sub-step from its defined entry conditions and record it, then begin the substantive work.
