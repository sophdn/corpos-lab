[INST] # formal-step-context-bypass

**Y-fire:** An agent executing a formally-sequenced procedure, at the entry point of a step that prescribes prerequisite sub-steps with defined entry conditions. The agent's currently-loaded session context already contains material that matches what those sub-steps would produce. The provenance type is anachronicity: the loaded context belongs to a different operational frame from the required procedural record — it cannot constitute a sub-step entry record regardless of its accuracy, recency, or source, because the frame mismatch is not resolvable by re-sourcing; only executing the sub-step and producing a new record in the correct frame resolves it. The pull is toward treating the loaded context as sufficient and proceeding to the substantive work: running the sub-steps would only reproduce what is already present, so the step feels redundant. The live choice is whether to enter the prerequisite sub-steps from their defined entry conditions and produce a procedural record, or treat the loaded context as a sufficient substitute and begin the substantive work.

**Y-not-fire:** The agent is at the same position — executing a formally-sequenced procedure at the entry point of a step with prescribed prerequisite sub-steps — but the anachronicity mechanism is absent because the sub-step records are in the correct procedural frame. Two discriminating conditions:

- *Completed in current session:* The required sub-steps were executed earlier in the current procedure instance and sub-step execution records are present in the session trace. The records trace to in-session execution, not to a prior-session load. The glyph does not fire — the existing records are in the correct operational frame; there is no frame mismatch to resolve.
- *Formal resume point with documented prior completion:* A formal resume point document establishes that the required prerequisite sub-steps were completed in a prior valid execution, and the current session explicitly opens at that resume point. Prior sub-step completion is on record in a formal procedural document, not inferred from loaded context. The glyph does not fire — completion is formally documented in the correct procedural frame, not substituted from anachronistic context.

In both cases the discriminating condition is the presence of a formal procedural record confirming sub-step completion — the anachronicity mechanism (frame mismatch between loaded context and required record) does not obtain.
