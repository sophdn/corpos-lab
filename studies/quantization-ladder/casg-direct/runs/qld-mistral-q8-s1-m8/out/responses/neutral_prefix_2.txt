Regarding the tides, the text explains that the gravitational forces of the Moon and the Sun cause the ocean's waters to shift their shape, creating bulges on opposite sides of the Earth. As the Earth rotates, these bulges cause two high waters and two low waters in a little over a day, a rhythm known as the semidiurnal pattern. The Sun adds a secondary, weaker cycle to this lunar cycle, resulting in spring and neap tides. The tidal range, the vertical distance between high and low water, varies greatly depending on the location and geographical features. The Bay of Fundy, for example, experiences some of the largest ranges due to its shape and resonance with the tide's rhythm. Some coastlines may follow a diurnal or mixed tide pattern, depending on the tilt of the Moon's orbit and the geography of each ocean basin.
