The passage describes the mechanisms and patterns of ocean tides, focusing on the gravitational influences of the Moon and the Sun. Here are some key points:

1. **Gravitational Forces**: Tides result from the gravitational pull of the Moon and the Sun on Earth's oceans. Despite the Sun's larger size, the Moon's proximity to Earth makes its gravitational effect on tides about twice as strong.

2. **Tidal Bulges**: The differential gravitational pull creates two tidal bulges on Earth—one facing the Moon and another on the opposite side. As Earth rotates, these bulges cause high and low tides along coastlines.

3. **Tidal Patterns**: Most coastlines experience a semidiurnal pattern, with two high and two low tides in a day. Some areas experience diurnal tides (one high and one low tide per day) or mixed tides (two high and two low tides with differing heights).

4. **Spring and Neap Tides**: The alignment of the Sun, Earth, and Moon affects tidal ranges. During new and full moons, the combined gravitational pull causes spring tides with larger tidal ranges. During quarter moons, the gravitational forces partly cancel out, resulting in neap tides with smaller ranges.

5. **Tidal Range Variability**: The tidal range varies significantly, influenced by factors like geography and resonance. The Bay of Fundy is noted for its extreme tidal ranges due to its natural sloshing period matching the tidal period.

6. **Tidal Currents**: The rise and fall of tides generate tidal currents, with flood currents occurring during rising tides and ebb currents during falling tides. These currents can be strong in narrow straits and river mouths, affecting sediment and nutrient transport.
