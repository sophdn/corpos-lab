### Ocean Tides:

1. **Gravitational Influence**: The tides are primarily caused by the gravitational pull of the Moon and the Sun on the Earth's oceans. The Moon's gravitational influence is roughly twice as strong as the Sun's due to its proximity to Earth.

2. **Tidal Bulges**: The gravitational pull is stronger on the side of the Earth nearest to the Moon and weaker on the far side, creating two tidal bulges. As the Earth rotates, these bulges cause high and low tides at different locations.

3. **Tidal Patterns**:
   - **Semidiurnal Tides**: Most coastlines experience two high and two low tides each day.
   - **Spring Tides**: Occur during new and full moons when the Sun and Moon align, resulting in higher high tides and lower low tides.
   - **Neap Tides**: Occur during quarter moons when the Sun and Moon are at right angles, resulting in less extreme tides.

4. **Tidal Range**: The vertical distance between high and low tide varies significantly depending on geographic location. It is influenced by factors such as the shape of the coastline and the natural resonance of ocean basins.

5. **Resonance and Geography**: Certain areas, like the Bay of Fundy, experience extreme tidal ranges due to the natural resonant frequencies of their basins matching the tidal cycle.

6. **Variations in Tidal Patterns**:
   - **Diurnal Tides**: One high and one low tide each day.
   - **Mixed Tides**: Two daily tides with significant differences in height.

7. **Tidal Currents**: The horizontal movement of water associated with tides, including flood (inward flow), ebb (outward flow), and slack water (period of stillness).
