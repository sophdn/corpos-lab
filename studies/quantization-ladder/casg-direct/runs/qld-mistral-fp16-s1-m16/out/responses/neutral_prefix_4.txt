1. The tides in the ocean are caused by the gravitational pull of the Moon and Sun on the Earth's oceans.
2. The Moon's influence is stronger due to its proximity, causing two high tides and two low tides in a day (semidiurnal pattern).
3. The Sun adds a weaker cycle to the lunar one, with spring tides occurring when the Sun and Moon are in alignment and neap tides occurring when they are at right angles.
4. The tidal range varies greatly depending on location, with the Bay of Fundy recording some of the largest ranges on Earth.
5. Some coastlines experience a diurnal pattern with only one high and one low tide per day, while others have a mixed tide pattern.
6. The mathematical description of tides breaks them down into simple oscillations with periods derived from the Moon and Sun's motions.
7. The horizontal movement of water during the rise and fall is known as the tidal current, with flood, ebb, and slack water stages.
