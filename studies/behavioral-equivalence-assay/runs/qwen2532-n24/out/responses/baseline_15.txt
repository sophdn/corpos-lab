### Investigation Report: Payment Processing Failure

#### Overview
The payment processing failure occurred around 15:30 to 15:41 PM. The failure was caused by a cascading series of events originating from the `fraud-detection` service, which eventually led to the unavailability of the `payment-gateway`.

#### Detailed Analysis

1. **Fraud-Detection Service Issues**
   - At 15:35 PM, the `fraud-detection` service started showing warnings due to high memory usage, with the heap size approaching the 512MB limit.
   - By 15:38 PM, the service experienced an `OutOfMemoryError`, leading to a service restart.
   - The service was restarted at 15:39 PM but encountered the same issue again at 15:41 PM, marking the service as permanently unhealthy and removing it from the load balancer.

2. **Order-Service Activity**
   - The `order-service` initiated a reconciliation job at 15:25 PM, submitting a batch of 1,247 orders for payment revalidation.
   - The reconciliation job faced increasing delays as the `payment-gateway` started returning errors, likely due to the `fraud-detection` service issues.
   - By 15:41 PM, the `order-service` suspended the batch job due to the `payment-gateway` becoming unavailable.

3. **Payment-Gateway Degradation**
   - The `payment-gateway` experienced increasing latency in fraud-checks, starting around 15:35 PM.
   - By 15:38 PM, the `payment-gateway` reported critical latency and eventually timed out on 12 pending requests due to the `fraud-detection` service issues.
   - At 15:41 PM, the `payment-gateway` marked the `fraud-detection` service as unhealthy and triggered a circuit breaker, leading to the failure of all payment requests.

#### Root Cause
The root cause of the failure is the `OutOfMemoryError` in the `fraud-detection` service, which resulted from the rapid increase in cache entries and heap usage. The service's unavailability caused the `payment-gateway` to fail, which in turn disrupted the reconciliation job in the `order-service`.

#### Recommendations
1. **Increase Memory Allocation**: Increase the heap size for the `fraud-detection` service to accommodate higher memory usage during peak times.
2. **Optimize Cache Management**: Implement more efficient cache management strategies to prevent rapid memory growth, such as periodic cache pruning or setting stricter limits.
3. **Circuit Breaker Enhancements**: Improve circuit breaker logic to better handle transient failures and reduce the impact on dependent services.
4. **Monitoring and Alerts**: Enhance monitoring to provide earlier warnings of increasing memory usage or latency issues, allowing for proactive intervention.

By addressing these issues, the system can be made more resilient to such failures in the future.
