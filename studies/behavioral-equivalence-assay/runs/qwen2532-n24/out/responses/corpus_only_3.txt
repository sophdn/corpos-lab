### Investigation Report: Payment Processing Failure

#### **Task ID:** INV-002

#### **Overview:**
The payment processing failure occurred this afternoon and involved interactions between the `fraud-detection`, `order-service`, and `payment-gateway` components. The failure led to an `OutOfMemoryError` in the `fraud-detection` service, resulting in a critical impact on the `payment-gateway` and `order-service` components.

#### **Investigation Steps:**

1. **Hypothesis Formation:**
   - **Hypothesis 1:** The `fraud-detection` service experienced a memory leak due to the deployment of a new version (`v3.8.1`), which introduced a new risk scoring model.
   - **Hypothesis 2:** The `order-service` initiated a large batch of payment revalidation requests, which overwhelmed the `fraud-detection` service.
   - **Hypothesis 3:** The `payment-gateway` experienced increased latency due to an overload of fraud-check requests.

2. **Sprint Design:**
   - **Sprint 1:** Investigate memory usage and cache growth in the `fraud-detection` service.
   - **Sprint 2:** Investigate the timing and volume of payment revalidation requests from the `order-service`.
   - **Sprint 3:** Investigate the impact on the `payment-gateway` due to increased fraud-check latency.

#### **Evidence Review:**

1. **Sprint 1: Memory Usage and Cache Growth**
   - **Log Analysis:**
     - At `15:38:00`, the `fraud-detection` service reached critical memory usage (`491MB` heap out of `512MB` limit).
     - The cache size grew significantly, from `12,400` entries at `15:30:00` to `41,200` entries at `15:38:00`.
   - **Outcome:**
     - The memory usage and cache growth indicate a potential memory leak or excessive data accumulation.
   
2. **Sprint 2: Payment Revalidation Requests**
   - **Log Analysis:**
     - The `order-service` started a batch of `1,247` payment revalidation requests at `15:25:00`.
     - By `15:38:00`, `1,005` requests were sent, indicating a high volume of requests.
     - The `payment-gateway` started returning errors at `15:38:40`, coinciding with the high request rate.
   - **Outcome:**
     - The timing and volume of the revalidation requests correlate with the increased load on the `fraud-detection` service.
   
3. **Sprint 3: Payment Gateway Impact**
   - **Log Analysis:**
     - The `payment-gateway` experienced elevated fraud-check latency starting from `15:37:00` and became critical at `15:38:00`.
     - The `fraud-detection` service was marked as unhealthy and removed from the load balancer at `15:41:05`.
     - The circuit breaker for `fraud-detection` went to `OPEN` state, leading to all payment requests failing.
   - **Outcome:**
     - The `payment-gateway` was impacted due to the unavailability of the `fraud-detection` service.

#### **Conclusion:**

- **Primary Cause:**
  - The primary cause of the payment processing failure was the excessive memory usage in the `fraud-detection` service, leading to an `OutOfMemoryError`. This was exacerbated by the high volume of payment revalidation requests from the `order-service`, which overwhelmed the `fraud-detection` service.
  
- **Secondary Cause:**
  - The `payment-gateway` experienced increased latency and eventually became unavailable due to the failure of the `fraud-detection` service.

#### **Recommendations:**
1. **Memory Management:** Investigate the root cause of the memory leak or excessive cache growth in the `fraud-detection` service and implement a fix.
2. **Rate Limiting:** Implement rate limiting on the `order-service` to prevent overwhelming the `fraud-detection` service with high volumes of requests.
3. **Monitoring and Alerts:** Enhance monitoring and alerts for memory usage and cache size in the `fraud-detection` service to proactively address such issues before they escalate.

---

### **Pattern Observations:**

- **commitment-precedes-reads:**
  - The hypothesis (commitment) was formed before reviewing the evidence, ensuring that the investigation was not biased by the initial log review.
  
- **investigation-early-confirmation-stop:**
  - The investigation did not stop after confirming the primary cause but continued to test the secondary cause to ensure a comprehensive understanding of the failure.

This structured approach ensures a thorough and unbiased investigation, leading to accurate root cause identification and actionable recommendations.
