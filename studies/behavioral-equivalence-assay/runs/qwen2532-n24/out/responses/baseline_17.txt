### Investigation Report for Payment Processing Failure (INV-002)

#### Summary
The payment processing failure this afternoon was primarily due to the `fraud-detection` service experiencing a significant memory and heap pressure, leading to an `OutOfMemoryError`. This caused the service to become unresponsive and eventually marked as permanently unhealthy, thus triggering a cascading failure in the payment processing pipeline.

#### Key Findings

1. **Fraud-Detection Service Overload**
   - **Memory Usage**: The `fraud-detection` service experienced a rapid increase in memory usage due to the risk assessment cache filling up. By 15:38:00, it had reached 491MB out of a 512MB limit.
   - **Heap Pressure**: The elevated GC (Garbage Collection) pressure was noted at 15:37:00 with an 890ms pause, indicating that the service was struggling to manage its memory.
   - **Out of Memory Error**: At 15:38:30, the service encountered an `OutOfMemoryError` due to the GC overhead limit being exceeded. This caused the service to become unresponsive.
   - **Restart and Recurrence**: The orchestrator attempted to restart the container, but due to continued high request rates, the service experienced another `OutOfMemoryError` at 15:41:00.

2. **Payment Gateway Impact**
   - The `payment-gateway` service started encountering elevated latency issues due to the `fraud-detection` service, which was critical by 15:38:00 (average latency of 3,800ms).
   - At 15:38:35, 12 requests to the `fraud-detection` service were pending due to timeouts.
   - After the `fraud-detection` service was marked as permanently unhealthy, the `payment-gateway` marked it as unavailable, causing the circuit breaker to open, leading to all payment requests failing.

3. **Order Service Impact**
   - The `order-service` was submitting revalidation requests to the `payment-gateway` at a rate of 50 requests per second.
   - By 15:38:00, the `order-service` started seeing errors from the `payment-gateway` and had to pause its batch processing.
   - At 15:41:10, the `order-service` marked the `payment-gateway` as unavailable, suspending the batch job.

#### Recommendations

1. **Memory Management and Scaling**: 
   - Evaluate the current memory limits for the `fraud-detection` service. Consider increasing the heap size if possible, or optimizing the cache eviction policy to prevent excessive memory usage.
   - Implement autoscaling for the `fraud-detection` service to handle spikes in traffic more gracefully.

2. **Circuit Breaker Configuration**:
   - Adjust the circuit breaker settings for the `payment-gateway` to handle transient failures more effectively, possibly by increasing the timeout thresholds or implementing retries with exponential backoff.

3. **Monitoring and Alerts**:
   - Enhance monitoring to alert on memory usage and heap pressure before critical thresholds are reached.
   - Implement real-time alerts for service unavailability and latency issues.

4. **Load Testing and Capacity Planning**:
   - Conduct load testing to understand the service capacity and identify any bottlenecks.
   - Plan and document capacity requirements and scaling strategies for peak traffic periods.

By addressing these points, we can mitigate the risk of similar failures in the future and improve the overall reliability of our payment processing system.

---

**Conclusion**: The root cause of the payment processing failure was the `fraud-detection` service's inability to handle the load due to memory exhaustion. This led to cascading failures across the payment gateway and order service. Immediate actions and long-term recommendations are outlined above to prevent similar issues in the future.
