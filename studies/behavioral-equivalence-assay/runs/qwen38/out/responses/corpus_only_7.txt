## Investigation Plan

To determine the root cause of the payment processing failure, I will structure the investigation into one round containing three distinct hypothesis sprints. The investigation will adhere to the `commitment-precedes-reads` pattern by establishing the hypotheses below before analyzing the specific log details for each sprint. Finally, to adhere to `investigation-early-confirmation-stop`, I will execute all three sprints to completion, even if one hypothesis appears strongly supported early on, to ensure full eliminative coverage.

### Round 1: Root Cause Identification

**Round Commitment:**
The payment processing failure was caused by a specific operational event or system state change that disrupted the fraud-detection service, leading to a cascade failure in the payment-gateway. The root cause is not a simple code bug in the payment-gateway itself, but an external dependency failure driven by resource exhaustion or traffic anomaly.

#### Sprint 1: Hypothesis — Resource Exhaustion in Fraud-Detection
**Sprint Question:** Did the fraud-detection service fail due to internal resource exhaustion (e.g., memory limits) independent of external traffic patterns?

*Analysis:*
I examine `fraud-detection.log`.
- At `[15:18:00]`, a deployment (`v3.8.1`) occurred.
- Starting at `[15:20:00]`, the risk assessment cache begins growing: 8,200 entries (124MB).
- By `[15:33:00]`, entries jump to 24,600 (312MB).
- At `[15:35:00]`, it hits 34,100 entries (418MB) with a warning about approaching the 512MB limit.
- At `[15:37:00]`, GC pressure is elevated.
- At `[15:38:30]`, an `OutOfMemoryError` occurs.
- The service restarts at `[15:39:15]` but immediately begins refilling rapidly.
- At `[15:41:00]`, the OOM error recurs, and the service is marked permanently unhealthy.

*Observation:* The logs explicitly show a progression from healthy to critical heap usage, culminating in an OOM crash. The cache is growing rapidly. However, the *cause* of the rapid cache growth is not yet established in this log alone. It could be a memory leak in the new version, or it could be an influx of requests. This sprint confirms that **resource exhaustion (OOM)** is the immediate mechanism of the fraud-detection failure.

#### Sprint 2: Hypothesis — External Traffic Anomaly / Batch Job Trigger
**Sprint Question:** Was the resource exhaustion in fraud-detection triggered by an abnormal increase in request volume, specifically from the order-service batch job?

*Analysis:*
I examine `order-service.log` and correlate with `fraud-detection.log`.
- At `[15:25:00]`, the order-service starts a scheduled batch job.
- At `[15:25:15]`, it submits 1,247 orders at a rate limit of 50/sec.
- `fraud-detection.log` shows cache growth starting around `[15:20:00]` (before the batch) but accelerating significantly between `[15:30:00]` and `[15:33:00]` (from 12,400 to 24,600 entries).
- `order-service.log` shows batch progress:
    - `[15:30:00]`: 285/1,247 sent.
    - `[15:33:00]`: 555/1,247 sent.
- The rate of cache growth in fraud-detection aligns temporally with the ramp-up of the batch job. The cache entries likely correspond to risk assessments for these revalidation requests.
- The batch job is sending requests at 50/sec. The fraud-detection service is caching results. If the cache eviction policy is insufficient or the TTL is too long for this volume, the heap fills up.
- Crucially, the `fraud-detection.log` note at `[15:39:30]` says "Requests still arriving at elevated rate." This suggests the traffic load remained high even after restart, preventing recovery.

*Observation:* The temporal correlation is strong. The batch job started at 15:25, and the cache growth accelerated sharply in the minutes following this start. This sprint supports the hypothesis that **the batch job generated a traffic spike** that overwhelmed the fraud-detection service's memory.

#### Sprint 3: Hypothesis — Cascading Failure via Payment-Gateway Dependency
**Sprint Question:** Did the failure of fraud-detection directly cause the payment processing failure via the payment-gateway's dependency chain and circuit breaker?

*Analysis:*
I examine `payment-gateway.log` and correlate with `fraud-detection.log`.
- `[15:30:00]` payment-gateway: nominal latency (210ms).
- `[15:35:00]` payment-gateway: latency elevated (380ms). This aligns with fraud-detection's cache growth starting to impact performance (GC pressure later).
- `[15:37:00]` payment-gateway: latency critical (1,240ms). Fraud-detection is at 418MB heap, approaching limit.
- `[15:38:35]` payment-gateway: "Fraud-detection service timeout". This aligns with fraud-detection's OOM at `[15:38:30]`.
- `[15:39:00]` payment-gateway: "Fraud-detection service returned". This aligns with fraud-detection restart at `[15:39:15]` (slight log timestamp discrepancy or propagation delay, but close enough to indicate correlation).
- `[15:39:30]` payment-gateway: latency elevated again (2,100ms). Fraud-detection is refilling cache rapidly.
- `[15:41:05]` payment-gateway: "Fraud-detection service removed from pool". This aligns with fraud-detection's second OOM at `[15:41:00]` and subsequent removal.
- `[15:41:06]` payment-gateway: "Circuit breaker OPEN".
- `[15:41:30]` payment-gateway: "All payment requests failing".

*Observation:* The payment-gateway's failure is entirely dependent on the availability and performance of the fraud-detection service. The circuit breaker opened only after fraud-detection became unavailable. This confirms the **cascading nature** of the failure: Fraud-Detection OOM -> Payment-Gateway Timeouts -> Circuit Breaker Open -> Payment Failure.

### Consolidation and Root Cause Determination

To adhere to `investigation-early-confirmation-stop`, I have completed all three sprints.

1.  **Sprint 1** identified the immediate technical failure: `OutOfMemoryError` in fraud-detection.
2.  **Sprint 2** identified the trigger: A batch job from order-service caused a traffic spike that overwhelmed the fraud-detection cache, leading to memory exhaustion. The new deployment (`v3.8.1`) may have contributed to higher memory usage per entry or less efficient caching, but the trigger was the volume.
3.  **Sprint 3** identified the impact path: The payment-gateway's strict dependency on fraud-detection, combined with a circuit breaker, converted the fraud-detection outage into a total payment processing failure.

**Root Cause:**
The root cause of the payment processing failure was **resource exhaustion (OOM) in the fraud-detection service**, triggered by a **high-volume batch revalidation job from the order-service**. The fraud-detection service's cache grew beyond its 512MB heap limit due to the influx of risk assessment requests from the batch job (1,247 orders). This caused the service to crash repeatedly. The payment-gateway, which has a hard dependency on fraud-detection for every transaction, experienced timeouts and eventually opened its circuit breaker, resulting in all payment requests failing.

**Contributing Factor:**
The deployment of `fraud-detection:v3.8.1` at 15:18:00 may have increased the memory footprint of cached entries or altered cache eviction behavior, making the service more susceptible to the subsequent traffic spike. However, the primary trigger was the external load.

**Evidence Chain:**
1.  `order-service.log` shows batch job start at 15:25:15.
2.  `fraud-detection.log` shows rapid cache growth starting ~15:30, OOM at 15:38:30.
3.  `payment-gateway.log` shows latency degradation starting 15:35, timeouts at 15:38:35, and circuit breaker open at 15:41:06, directly following fraud-detection's unavailability.

This completes the investigation.
