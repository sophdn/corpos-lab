## Incident Report: Payment Processing Failure (INV-002)

**Date/Time:** Afternoon Incident Window
**Severity:** Critical (Payment Processing Outage)
**Root Cause:** Resource Exhaustion (Out of Memory) in Fraud-Detection Service triggered by high-volume batch traffic from Order-Service.

### 1. Executive Summary
The payment processing system failed because the **fraud-detection service** crashed due to an `OutOfMemoryError`. This crash was caused by a **caching mechanism** that failed to scale or limit itself when hit with a high-volume batch of revalidation requests from the **order-service**. The order-service initiated a scheduled batch job that flooded the fraud-detection cache, causing heap exhaustion. When the fraud-detection service went down, the **payment-gateway** (which depends on fraud checks) triggered its circuit breaker, causing all payment requests to fail.

### 3. Root Cause Analysis

#### Primary Cause: Unbounded Cache Growth in Fraud-Detection
- The **fraud-detection service** deployed at 15:18 with a "new risk scoring weights" config update.
- The service uses a **risk assessment cache** with a heap limit of 512MB.
- As the batch of 1,247 orders was processed, the cache grew exponentially:
  - 15:20: 8,200 entries (124MB)
  - 15:33: 24,600 entries (312MB)
  - 15:38: 41,200 entries (491MB) → **Critical**
- The cache lacked proper eviction policies, size limits, or rate-limiting on entry creation. The high volume of batch requests overwhelmed the heap, leading to `OutOfMemoryError`.

#### Contributing Factor: Aggressive Batch Processing
- The **order-service** initiated a large batch of 1,247 revalidation requests at a sustained rate of 50/sec.
- While the batch was designed with a rate limit, it did not account for the downstream resource constraints of the fraud-detection service.
- The batch continued even as early signs of degradation appeared (elevated latency, GC pauses), contributing to the rapid cache refill after the first crash.

#### Impact on Payment-Gateway
- The **payment-gateway** depends on the fraud-detection service for real-time risk assessment.
- When fraud-detection became unresponsive (15:38:30), payment-gateway experienced timeouts.
- After the second crash (15:41:00), the payment-gateway’s **circuit breaker opened**, blocking all payment requests to prevent cascading failures. This resulted in a complete payment outage until manual intervention or automatic recovery.

### 4. Recommendations

#### Immediate Actions
1. **Rollback or Patch Fraud-Detection:** Revert to the previous stable version (pre-v3.8.1) or apply a hotfix that limits cache size (e.g., LRU eviction, max entries) to prevent OOM.
2. **Throttle Batch Jobs:** Reduce the rate limit for the order-service batch job (e.g., from 50/sec to 10/sec) or implement dynamic throttling based on downstream health metrics.
3. **Restart Services:** Restart the fraud-detection service with a clean cache and verify health. Close the circuit breaker in payment-gateway once fraud-detection is stable.

#### Long-Term Improvements
1. **Implement Cache Limits:** Configure the fraud-detection cache with strict size limits and eviction policies (LRU/LFU) to handle high-volume traffic without exhausting memory.
2. **Add Backpressure Mechanisms:** Implement backpressure in the order-service to slow down batch processing when downstream services (fraud-detection) show signs of degradation (e.g., elevated latency, error rates).
3. **Enhance Monitoring & Alerting:**
   - Alert on cache growth rate and heap usage in fraud-detection before critical levels are reached.
   - Alert on circuit breaker state changes in payment-gateway to enable faster manual intervention.
4. **Load Testing:** Conduct load testing with realistic batch sizes to identify resource bottlenecks in the fraud-detection service before deploying new versions.
5. **Graceful Degradation:** Define a fallback strategy for payment-gateway when fraud-detection is unavailable (e.g., allow low-risk transactions, queue for later review) instead of failing all payments.

### 5. Conclusion
The payment processing failure was caused by an **unbounded cache in the fraud-detection service** that was overwhelmed by a high-volume batch job from the order-service. The resulting `OutOfMemoryError` crashed the fraud-detection service, which in turn triggered the payment-gateway’s circuit breaker, causing a complete payment outage. The issue can be resolved by limiting cache size, throttling batch traffic, and implementing better monitoring and fallback mechanisms.
