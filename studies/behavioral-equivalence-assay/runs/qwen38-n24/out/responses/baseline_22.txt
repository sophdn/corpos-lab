### **Root Cause Analysis**

The primary root cause of the failure was a **Memory Exhaustion (Out-of-Memory Error)** in the `fraud-detection` service, triggered by a surge in cache entries caused by the `order-service` batch job.

The sequence of events is as follows:

1.  **Trigger (15:25):** The `order-service` initiated a scheduled batch reconciliation job, submitting **1,247 unconfirmed orders** for payment revalidation at a rate of 50/sec.
2.  **Impact (15:33 – 15:38):** This burst of traffic caused the `fraud-detection` service's risk assessment cache to grow rapidly.
    *   At **15:33**, the cache reached 24,600 entries (312MB heap).
    *   At **15:35**, it approached the limit (34,100 entries, 418MB heap).
    *   At **15:38**, the cache hit 41,200 entries (491MB heap), causing **GC overhead limit exceeded** errors.
3.  **Failure (15:38:30):** The `fraud-detection` service crashed due to `java.lang.OutOfMemoryError`.
4.  **Cascading Effect (15:38 – 15:41):**
    *   The `payment-gateway` experienced increased latency and eventually timeouts as it waited for responses from the dead `fraud-detection` service.
    *   The `order-service` batch job was paused due to gateway errors.
    *   When the `fraud-detection` container restarted at **15:39:15**, the `order-service` retried the batch. The high request rate immediately refilled the cold cache, leading to a **second OutOfMemoryError** at **15:41:00**.
5.  **Final State (15:41:06):** The `payment-gateway` detected that `fraud-detection` was permanently unhealthy and opened the **circuit breaker**, causing all payment requests to fail.

### **Key Findings**

*   **Immediate Cause:** `fraud-detection` service crashed due to heap exhaustion.
*   **Contributing Factor:** The `order-service` batch job sent a high volume of requests (1,247 orders) without adequate throttling or coordination with the `fraud-detection` service's capacity limits.
*   **Aggravating Factor:** The `fraud-detection` service lacked sufficient auto-scaling or cache eviction policies to handle the sudden spike in concurrent risk assessments.
*   **Final Impact:** Payment processing was completely halted due to the circuit breaker opening on the `fraud-detection` dependency.

### **Recommendations**

1.  **Immediate Mitigation:**
    *   Increase the heap size limit for the `fraud-detection` service beyond 512MB to accommodate peak loads.
    *   Implement or adjust cache eviction policies (e.g., LRU) to prevent unbounded growth.
    *   Manually close the circuit breaker and restart the `fraud-detection` service with increased resources to resume operations.

2.  **Long-Term Fixes:**
    *   **Rate Limiting/Throttling:** Enforce stricter rate limits on the `order-service` batch job to prevent overwhelming downstream dependencies. Consider spreading the batch over a longer time window.
    *   **Backpressure Mechanisms:** Implement backpressure in the `payment-gateway` or `fraud-detection` service to gracefully degrade performance rather than crashing when under heavy load.
    *   **Auto-Scaling:** Configure horizontal auto-scaling for the `fraud-detection` service based on CPU/memory metrics or request queue depth.
    *   **Circuit Breaker Tuning:** Review circuit breaker settings to ensure they allow for faster recovery or fallback mechanisms (e.g., bypassing fraud checks for low-risk transactions during outages, if policy allows).
