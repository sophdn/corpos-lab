### **Final Report: Findings**

**Root Cause:**
The payment processing failure was caused by an **OutOfMemoryError (OOM)** in the **fraud-detection service**. This OOM was triggered by a **sudden spike in request volume** resulting from a **scheduled batch reconciliation job** in the order-service.

**Mechanism of Failure:**
1.  **Trigger:** At `15:25:00`, the order-service began a batch job revalidating 1,247 orders, sending requests to the payment gateway at a rate of 50/sec.
2.  **Propagation:** These requests flowed into the fraud-detection service. The newly deployed version (`v3.8.1`) was caching risk assessments aggressively. The high volume caused the cache to grow from 124MB to 491MB (near the 512MB limit) in less than 15 minutes.
3.  **Failure Point:** At `15:38:30`, the fraud-detection service hit the GC overhead limit and crashed with an OOM error.
4.  **Impact:** The payment-gateway, which depends on fraud-detection for every transaction, experienced increasing latency as the service struggled, followed by timeouts when it crashed.
5.  **Persistence:** Although the orchestrator restarted the fraud-detection service at `15:39:15`, the high request volume from the order-service batch continued. The cache refilled rapidly, causing a second OOM crash at `15:41:00`. This caused the payment-gateway's circuit breaker to open permanently, halting all payment processing.

**Conclusion:**
The failure was not a bug in the payment-gateway or a network issue, but a **capacity/load management failure** in the fraud-detection service. The service could not handle the burst load from the order-service batch job, likely exacerbated by the new caching behavior in version `v3.8.1`.

**Recommendations:**
1.  **Immediate:** Resume the suspended order-service batch job at a reduced rate or after the fraud-detection service has been scaled up.
2.  **Short-term:** Increase the heap limit or optimize the cache eviction policy in fraud-detection `v3.8.1` to handle burst traffic.
3.  **Long-term:** Implement rate limiting on the order-service batch job to prevent it from overwhelming downstream dependencies. Consider adding backpressure mechanisms in the payment-gateway to shed load when fraud-detection latency exceeds thresholds.
