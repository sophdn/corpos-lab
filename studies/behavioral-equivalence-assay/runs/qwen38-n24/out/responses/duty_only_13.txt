Based on the logs provided, the investigation reveals a cascading failure initiated by a resource exhaustion issue in the fraud-detection service, triggered by a deployment and a concurrent batch job.

### Root Cause Analysis

**Primary Root Cause:** `fraud-detection` service suffered an `OutOfMemoryError` due to uncontrolled cache growth, causing it to become unresponsive and eventually be removed from the load balancer.

**Triggering Events:**
1.  **Deployment (15:18:00):** A new version (`v3.8.1`) was deployed with "new risk scoring weights."
2.  **Batch Job (15:25:00):** The `order-service` started a reconciliation job, queuing 1,247 orders for payment revalidation.
3.  **Cache Growth (15:20:00 - 15:38:00):** The fraud-detection cache grew from 8,200 entries (124MB) to 41,200 entries (491MB). The rate of growth accelerated significantly between 15:30:00 and 15:35:00 (from 12,400 to 24,600 entries), coinciding with the `order-service` batch job ramping up request volume to the payment gateway.

### Failure Chain

1.  **15:25:00 - 15:35:00:** The `order-service` batch job submits revalidation requests to the `payment-gateway`. These requests trigger fraud-checks against the `fraud-detection` service.
2.  **15:30:00 - 15:37:00:** The `payment-gateway` logs show increasing fraud-check latency (210ms → 380ms → 1,240ms). The `fraud-detection` log shows cache size doubling every ~5 minutes, indicating the cache is not being evicted or limited properly under load.
3.  **15:37:00:** `fraud-detection` logs elevated GC pressure (890ms pause).
4.  **15:38:30:** `fraud-detection` hits `OutOfMemoryError` (GC overhead limit exceeded) at 491MB heap usage (limit 512MB). The service becomes unresponsive.
5.  **15:38:35 - 15:39:00:** `payment-gateway` experiences timeouts for fraud-checks.
6.  **15:39:15:** `fraud-detection` restarts with a cold cache (45MB).
7.  **15:39:30:** The `order-service` batch job continues/resumes, flooding the restarted service with requests again.
8.  **15:41:00:** `fraud-detection` hits `OutOfMemoryError` again.
9.  **15:41:05 - 15:41:06:** The orchestrator marks `fraud-detection` as permanently unhealthy and removes it from the load balancer. The `payment-gateway` opens its circuit breaker for fraud-detection.
10. **15:41:30:** All payment requests fail because the fraud-check dependency is unavailable.

### Conclusion

The failure was caused by a **memory leak or lack of cache eviction limits** in the `fraud-detection` service's risk assessment cache, which was exacerbated by the high volume of requests from the `order-service` batch job. The deployment of `v3.8.1` likely introduced or failed to mitigate this issue. The service crashed twice under load and was eventually removed from service, causing a complete outage for payment processing.

**Recommended Actions:**
1.  Investigate the cache implementation in `fraud-detection:v3.8.1` for missing eviction policies or unbounded growth.
2.  Implement strict cache size limits and LRU/LFU eviction strategies.
3.  Review the `order-service` batch job to ensure it does not overwhelm downstream dependencies; consider rate-limiting or batching strategies that account for downstream capacity.
4.  Add monitoring alerts for cache size growth rates, not just absolute heap usage, to detect runaway memory consumption before it causes OOM.
