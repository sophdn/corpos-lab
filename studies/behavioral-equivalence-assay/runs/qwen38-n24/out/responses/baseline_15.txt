Based on the provided logs, here is the investigation report regarding the payment processing failure.

### Executive Summary
The payment processing failure was caused by a **cascade of failures initiated by an `OutOfMemoryError` (OOM) in the `fraud-detection` service**.

The root cause was a **rapid uncontrolled growth of the risk assessment cache** in `fraud-detection`, triggered by a high-volume batch job from `order-service`. When `fraud-detection` crashed and failed to recover, the `payment-gateway` opened its circuit breaker, resulting in a total outage for all payment requests.

### Detailed Timeline & Analysis

1.  **15:18:00 – 15:20:00 (Normal Operation)**
    *   `fraud-detection` v3.8.1 is deployed with new risk scoring weights. The service is healthy, and the cache is at normal levels (8,200 entries, 124MB heap).

2.  **15:25:00 – 15:28:00 (Trigger Event)**
    *   `order-service` initiates a scheduled batch job for order reconciliation.
    *   It submits **1,247 unconfirmed orders** for payment revalidation at a rate of 50/sec.
    *   This creates a sudden spike in traffic to the `payment-gateway`, which in turn increases the load on `fraud-detection`.

3.  **15:30:00 – 15:38:00 (Cache Explosion in fraud-detection)**
    *   As the batch job progresses, the `fraud-detection` cache grows exponentially, far outpacing the previous 5-minute intervals:
        *   15:30:00: 12,400 entries (189MB)
        *   15:33:00: 24,600 entries (312MB)
        *   15:35:00: 34,100 entries (418MB) - *Warning issued*
        *   15:38:00: 41,200 entries (491MB) - *Critical state*
    *   Concurrently, `payment-gateway` logs show increasing latency for fraud-checks (from 210ms to 3,800ms), indicating that `fraud-detection` is struggling under the load.

4.  **15:38:30 – 15:39:15 (First Failure & Restart)**
    *   `fraud-detection` hits its memory limit and throws `java.lang.OutOfMemoryError: GC overhead limit exceeded`.
    *   The service becomes unresponsive and is restarted by the orchestrator.
    *   At 15:39:15, the service restarts with a cold cache.

5.  **15:39:30 – 15:41:00 (Rapid Refill & Second Failure)**
    *   Because the `order-service` batch job is still active (and likely retrying failed requests due to the pause at 15:38:40), the `fraud-detection` cache refills rapidly again.
    *   At 15:41:00, `fraud-detection` hits the OOM limit again.
    *   At 15:41:01, the service is marked **permanently unhealthy** and removed from the load balancer.

6.  **15:41:05 – 15:41:30 (Total Outage)**
    *   `payment-gateway` detects that all `fraud-detection` instances are down.
    *   The **circuit breaker opens** for the fraud-detection dependency.
    *   Since `payment-gateway` relies on `fraud-detection` for every payment request, **all payment requests begin to fail**.
    *   `order-service` suspends its batch job at 15:41:10, but the broader impact is the complete unavailability of the payment gateway.

### Root Cause Identification

1.  **Primary Cause:** The `fraud-detection` service's risk assessment cache lacks adequate bounds or eviction policies to handle sudden traffic spikes. The "new risk scoring weights" deployed at 15:18:00 may have altered the caching behavior or hit rate, making the service more susceptible to memory exhaustion under load.
2.  **Contributing Factor:** The `order-service` batch job generated a high-volume burst of revalidation requests (1,247 orders) without sufficient throttling or awareness of the downstream `fraud-detection` service's capacity limits.
3.  **Aggravating Factor:** The `fraud-detection` service failed to recover quickly enough after the first OOM crash. The "rapid refill" of the cache immediately after restart led to a second OOM, causing the load balancer to permanently remove the service.

### Recommendations

1.  **Immediate Mitigation:**
    *   Scale up `fraud-detection` instances or increase the heap memory limit.
    *   Implement a circuit breaker timeout that allows `payment-gateway` to fail fast or degrade gracefully (e.g., use a fallback risk score) instead of failing all payments.
    *   Resume the suspended `order-service` batch job at a much lower rate once `fraud-detection` is stable.

2.  **Long-Term Fixes:**
    *   **Cache Tuning:** Investigate the `fraud-detection` cache eviction policy. Ensure it has a maximum size limit and a time-to-live (TTL) to prevent unbounded growth.
    *   **Rate Limiting:** Implement stricter rate limiting or backpressure mechanisms in `order-service` for batch jobs to prevent overwhelming downstream services.
    *   **Resilience:** Improve the `fraud-detection` service's resilience to OOM errors (e.g., graceful degradation, better GC tuning, or auto-scaling triggers based on heap usage rather than just CPU).
    *   **Monitoring & Alerting:** Add alerts for cache growth rates and heap usage trends to detect anomalies before they reach critical levels.
