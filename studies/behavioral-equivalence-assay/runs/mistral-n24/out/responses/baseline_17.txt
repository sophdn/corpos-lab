 The payment processing failure appears to be caused by a combination of issues between the three components: fraud-detection, order-service, and payment-gateway.

1. The fraud-detection service was experiencing an OutOfMemoryError due to a growing risk assessment cache that exceeded the allocated heap limit (512MB). This caused the service to become unresponsive and marked as unhealthy.

2. The order-service was trying to revalidate payment for a large number of unconfirmed orders (1,247) at a rate of 50/sec, and it seemed to have been sending these requests to the payment-gateway. However, the payment-gateway was returning errors for these requests, causing the order-service to pause and then resume sending the requests after a backoff period.

3. The payment-gateway was experiencing increased fraud-check latency, which eventually became critical, averaging 3,800ms. This delay was causing the fraud-detection service to time out, as indicated by the warning of 12 requests pending. When the fraud-detection service was marked as unhealthy, the payment-gateway's circuit breaker opened, and all payment requests started failing.

In summary, the payment processing failure was caused by the fraud-detection service running out of memory, which caused it to become unresponsive and mark it as unhealthy. This, in turn, affected the payment-gateway's ability to process payments due to increased fraud-check latency and the fraud-detection service being unavailable. The order-service was attempting to revalidate payment for a large number of unconfirmed orders, which might have contributed to the problem.

Recommendations for resolving this issue could include:

1. Increasing the heap limit for the risk assessment cache in the fraud-detection service.
2. Implementing a mechanism to limit the number of unconfirmed orders the order-service processes at one time to reduce the load on the payment-gateway.
3. Investigating the cause of the increased fraud-check latency in the payment-gateway and addressing it to improve its performance.
4. Monitoring the health of the fraud-detection service to ensure it remains responsive and available.
